# 2024全国I卷阅读C篇

## 原文

The rapid development of artificial intelligence has sparked debates in the field of education. While some embrace its potential to personalize learning and automate routine tasks, others fear it may undermine traditional pedagogy and diminish the human connection that is central to effective teaching.

Proponents argue that AI can identify individual learning gaps more efficiently than any teacher, tailoring instruction to each student's pace and style. Adaptive learning platforms, for instance, analyze thousands of data points to recommend targeted exercises, allowing students to progress at their own speed. This approach, supporters claim, frees teachers to focus on higher-order thinking skills, mentorship, and social-emotional development.

Critics, however, raise concerns about equity and dependency. Not all students have equal access to technology, which could exacerbate existing educational inequalities. Moreover, an over-reliance on AI-driven assessments may reduce education to measurable outputs, neglecting creativity, critical thinking, and ethical reasoning—qualities that resist easy quantification.

Perhaps the most nuanced concern is the erosion of teacher agency. When algorithms dictate curriculum pacing and content, educators may feel reduced to facilitators of a system they did not design and cannot meaningfully modify. This shift risks producing students who are skilled at navigating AI interfaces but poorly prepared for the ambiguity of real-world problem-solving.

A balanced approach is therefore essential. Technology should serve as a tool that augments rather than replaces human judgment. Schools that succeed will be those that integrate AI thoughtfully, maintaining the relational and ethical dimensions of education while leveraging data-driven insights to support every learner.
